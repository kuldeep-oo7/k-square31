WHAT I SET OUT TO DO AND WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED

The plan for this session was: restore the tail-based rung gate, re-validate the
1% stop / 3% target geometry, measure participation, then build either the
volatility-normalised grid (A-4) or the cross-sectional residual ladder (A-3).

I re-validated the geometry first. It collapsed. Everything below follows from
that, and the build I shipped is not the build I planned.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE THREE CONTROLS THE REPO DID NOT HAVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

weekly-cycle.js judges ONE SYMBOL AT A TIME. That is correct for a trading
decision and useless for the question "does this rule work". Three things were
therefore unmeasurable, and I built them:

  (a) A POOLED PANEL cut on shared calendar dates. Per-symbol splits let one
      name's label density move another name's test window.

  (b) A BUY-AND-HOLD CONTROL over the identical names and identical windows.
      Without it a rule measured in a bull market reports the market's drift as
      its own edge. The repo had no benchmark anywhere.

  (c) A SELECTION HURDLE SCORED ON THE TRIALS ACTUALLY RUN. The per-symbol gate
      uses sqrt(2*ln(slots+1)) = 1.89, correct for a 6-trial search. Run that
      same gate across 97 names on 2 sides and you have run a 194-trial search,
      where the bar is 2.76. Counting only the trials one function can see is
      how a universe-wide sweep passes on noise while every individual test
      looks disciplined.

Shipped as api/_lib/panel.js + scripts/panel-study.js + scripts/test-panel.js,
wired into `npm run check`. Nothing in the trading path changed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE FINDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

97 NSE large caps, 5 years (2021-09-15 .. 2026-09-15), long side, current
continuous engine (20-session time stop, 5 concurrent, cohort censoring,
gap-aware exits), 24 stop/target geometries.

  Reproduce with:  node scripts/panel-study.js --fetch
                   node scripts/panel-study.js

THE CONTROL — equal-weighted buy-and-hold of the same 97 names:

    development   730 sessions   +153.0% per name
    validation    248 sessions     +4.3% per name
    final test    248 sessions     +2.0% per name

THE GRID — 0 of 24 configurations survive validation. ALL 24 VALIDATION CELLS
ARE NEGATIVE.

    shipped 2% stop / 2% target :  dev +0.0674%   val -0.1529% (t -1.14)
    best on development 3% / 4% :  dev +0.2808%   val -0.1742% (t -0.68)
                                   (t 2.18)

Read the development column against the control. The ladder looks best exactly
where the market rose 153%, and goes negative where the market went flat. On
this evidence the long ladder is a LEVERED LONG BETA EXPRESSION, not an edge: it
pays when the index runs and does not pay otherwise. That is a timing bet on
direction, dressed as a mechanical entry rule.

THE CONDITIONERS — 0 of 6 survive. I profiled 12-1 momentum, 5-day reversal,
21-day relative strength vs the cross-section, distance from the 52-week high,
ATR%, and volume ratio. Every one flips sign between development and validation.
The two strongest in development are the two that flip hardest. That is the
textbook signature of overfitting, and it means ranking does not rescue the rule.

THE SHORT SIDE IS WORSE, AND IT IS THE CLEANEST RESULT IN THE STUDY.

    node scripts/panel-study.js --side short

All 24 geometries are negative in ALL THREE SPLITS, at overwhelming significance:

    stop/target   development           validation          final test
    0.75 / 1.5    -0.2281%  t -11.12    -0.2165%  t -4.26   -0.1564%  t -3.15
    1.00 / 1.5    -0.2631%  t -10.28    -0.2192%  t -3.45   -0.1501%  t -2.46
    2.00 / 2.0    -0.3560%  t  -7.73    -0.2632%  t -2.12   -0.0932%  t -0.80
    3.00 / 4.0    -0.5558%  t  -5.58    -0.2303%  t -0.88   -0.0718%  t -0.31

This is not "no edge". This is a measured, reliably NEGATIVE expectancy, and it
is the only thing in five years of this data that is statistically unambiguous.
A development t of -11.12 is not noise.

The reason is structural and not fixable by tuning: the short ladder SELLS
RALLIES INTO A MARKET WITH POSITIVE DRIFT. The same universe rose 153%, 4.3% and
2.0% across the three windows. Shorting strength in a market that trends up
loses money by construction, and the wider the stop the more it loses
(-0.23% at a 0.75% stop, -0.56% at a 3% stop in development) because a wider
stop simply holds the losing short longer.

Note the sign asymmetry against the long side: long geometries get BETTER as the
stop widens, short geometries get WORSE. That is the same drift term showing up
with opposite signs, and it is further evidence that what the ladder is
measuring is market direction rather than any property of the entry rule.

The shipped gate approved ZERO short ladders out of 97 names, so this has not
cost money either. But `weeklyCycle` still evaluates and ranks the short side,
and `verdict()` will select it if it ever passes. On this evidence the short
ladder should not be tradeable at all. That is a trading-rule change, so it is
flagged in section 7 rather than made here.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THINGS I SAID EARLIER THAT ARE WRONG. I AM RETRACTING THEM.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RETRACTION 1 — "1% stop / 3% target: +0.1894% per fill, cluster-robust t = 3.14,
4/4 folds positive."

  Does not replicate. On the current engine 1%/3% gives dev +0.0114%,
  validation -0.1504% (t -1.65), final +0.0345%. The original number came from
  the old friday-flat engine with unbounded holds. I carried it across an engine
  change without re-deriving it. That was my error.

RETRACTION 2 — Appendix B-0: "hit rate and P&L move in OPPOSITE directions,
monotonically, across every single cell."

  Not true on the current engine. With a 20-session time stop the two move in
  the SAME direction: wider stops raise both hit rate (27% -> 64%) and per-fill
  P&L. The inversion was an artifact of forced Friday liquidation, which booked
  losers before mean reversion could act.

CONSEQUENCE — I DID NOT RESTORE THE TAIL GATE, and it should not be restored.
Its thresholds (maxRungCvar95Pct 6.0, minWinToWorstLossRatio 0.30) were
calibrated against the friday-flat distribution. Re-deriving them on the current
engine, as I set out to do, shows the premise they rested on no longer holds.
Restoring them would have shipped two more magic numbers with veto power into a
codebase whose central defect is exactly that. I was wrong to have it queued as
the top priority.

ALSO CORRECTED — I wrote in panel.js that sqrt(2*ln N) "under-penalises" small
grids. Backwards. It sits ABOVE the exact expected maximum at every practical N
(at N=6: 1.8930 vs 1.3001), so the existing hurdle errs strict. Fixed in the
source before commit.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. A-4 SHOULD NOT BE BUILT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Fill rate by trailing-ATR decile, shipped config:

    decile 1 (ATR 0.86-1.69%)   7.9% of rungs filled   avg cohort net +0.0037%
    decile 5 (ATR 2.28-2.47%)  14.8%                          -0.0047%
    decile 10 (ATR 3.92%+)     29.5%                          -0.0016%

    overall: 0.812 fills per cohort of 5 rungs

Volatility-normalising the grid WOULD equalise participation — the fill-rate
slope is steep and monotone. But the P&L column is flat noise across all ten
deciles, and the highest-participation decile is negative. A-4 buys more fills
in a rule whose per-fill expectancy is zero. More fills x zero edge = zero, plus
more cost and more tail. Do not build it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT THE SHIPPED SYSTEM GETS RIGHT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STAGED EXITS WERE DELETED, NOT FLAGGED OFF. Commit 86467f8 removed
STAGED_EXIT_3_TIER, depthProfile, exitPolicy and comparePolicies outright, and
scripts/verify-ci-flags.js now asserts their absence: "unvalidated staged exits
and promised holding horizons are absent". A shipped, user-visible feature was
removed because it was not measured. That is rarer than any fix in this repo and
it is the discipline the first counter-audit asked for.

THE GATE IS GENUINELY CONSERVATIVE. Across 97 names x 2 sides it approves 3.

    SBILIFE    long L1   validation 23 fills +0.087%   final 21 fills +0.557%
    TORNTPHARM long L1   validation 25 fills +0.793%   final 18 fills +0.166%
    TVSMOTOR   long L2   validation 11 fills +0.102%   final  5 fills -1.284%

    approved: 3 of 194 (1.5%). Pure chance at a 5% per-test rate gives ~10.

The gate rejects MORE than chance would. Its default behaviour is "do not
trade", which is the correct behaviour for a rule with no measured edge. This is
a real strength and it is why the absence of edge has not cost money.

But n=3 is not a portfolio, and those 3 clear a 6-trial hurdle (1.89) when the
search that produced them was 194 trials (bar 2.76). Their mean on the untouched
final test is -0.187% per fill, carried by TVSMOTOR's 5 fills. Three names is a
statistical accident either way.

OTHER GENUINE UPGRADES IN 86467f8, verified: three-way dev/validation/final-test
split with purged boundary cohorts; splits computed on input dates rather than
future-dependent label counts; maxHoldingSessions 20; maxConcurrentPositions 5;
cohort censoring via the `complete` flag; gap-aware exits (target-gap, STOP_GAP);
block SE honestly labelled a research approximation.

Note on the lowerPct >= 45 gate: I earlier called this a regression. It is not.
My tail-gate version never left my own working tree, so this was never removed
from the shipped line of development. It is a parallel-development divergence,
and the divergence is now moot given section 3.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. ONE DEFECT FOUND AND FIXED IN NEW CODE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

deflatedTStat(null, N) returned -2.14 instead of null, because Number(null) is 0
and Number.isFinite(0) is true. clusterRobustMean legitimately returns a null
t-statistic whenever a sample holds fewer than two clusters, so the path is
reached in normal use — it would have fabricated a confident "no edge" from
absent data. Caught by its own test, fixed before commit.

Also tightened: quintileProfile originally called a conditioner sign-stable when
its spreads merely shared a sign. Spreads of -0.020, -0.200 and -0.011 share a
sign and say nothing. Stability now additionally requires each window's spread
to exceed that window's own cluster-robust sampling error. Under the old rule
5-day reversal reported stable; it now correctly reports unstable.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT IS STILL OPEN, AND WHAT IS YOURS TO DECIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MINE, NOT DONE YET:
  - A-3 (cross-sectional residual ladder + beta hedge) is now the ONLY untested
    structural idea left. Section 2 is the argument for it: if the ladder is
    levered beta, then removing beta is the one change that could expose whether
    anything idiosyncratic is underneath. It needs benchmark data in the research
    path, which does not exist yet.
  - Live ATR-derived stops (1.42%, 1.55% observed on the portal) still diverge
    from the flat 2.0% the backtest measures. The panel measures a rule nobody
    trades.
  - Backtest runs on cash bars for a futures strategy. No ADV/OI/spread filter,
    no capacity curve.
  - metaLabelConfidence still holds hard veto power while untrained.

YOURS, NOT MINE:
  - Whether the short side should be disabled outright. This is the strongest
    recommendation in this document and the only one backed by significance
    rather than by absence of it: 24 of 24 short geometries lose in all three
    splits, development t reaching -11.12. The gate currently rejects every
    short candidate, so nothing is bleeding today, but the capability is live.
    I have not touched it, because removing a side is a trading-rule change.
  - stopLossPct stays at 2.0. I did not change it. No geometry in the grid has a
    confirmed edge, so there is no measured basis to move it, and the trading
    rule is yours.
  - Whether the plan should refuse to emit at all while the panel reports
    NO_CONFIRMED_EDGE. I have deliberately NOT wired panelVerdict into the live
    path. It is computed and reported only. Wiring it in would be the single
    largest behaviour change in the system's history — it would likely stop all
    trading — and adding it switched-off by default would be exactly the
    fake-fix pattern this audit exists to catch. So it is a decision, and it is
    yours.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. THE BLUNT VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The fixed-percentage weekly ladder has no measurable out-of-sample edge on a
97-name NSE large-cap panel over 5 years: not at any of 24 geometries, not
conditioned on any of 6 standard cross-sectional factors. Its development-period
performance tracks a 153% bull market.

The system around it is honest — the gate refuses 98.5% of candidates, the
unvalidated features were deleted rather than flagged off, the splits are purged,
the costs are real and dated. The machinery is sound. The engine is sound.

What is missing is a reason to be long this name, this week. Fundamentals,
cross-sectional ranking, regime, and residualisation are all still absent from
the decision, and the price-mechanical rule alone does not carry the weight.
That is the gap between what this is and what you asked for, and no amount of
tuning the ladder closes it.

The measurement apparatus to tell the difference now exists and runs in CI. That
is what this round delivered.
