C-5  UNTRAINED MAGIC NUMBERS HOLD HARD VETO POWER; THE ONE HONESTLY-MEASURED
     MODEL IS SIDELINED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The most conceptually serious finding, and invisible to the test suite because
the tests assert that the gate FIRES, never that its coefficients MEAN anything.

metaLabelConfidence is documented as "Meta-Labeling: Secondary Machine Learning
Quality Control Gate (Lopez de Prado)." It is a hand-written logistic function:

    let z = -0.2;
    z += (primaryScore / 100) * 1.8;
    if (hurst <= 0.45) z += 0.5;
    if (vpin >= 0.65) z -= 1.8;
    if (er >= 0.40) z += 0.4;
    const metaProb = 1 / (1 + Math.exp(-z));
    const decision = (metaProb >= 0.50 && vpin < 0.65) ? "TAKE" : "VETO";

Not one of those coefficients was fitted to anything. Meta-labelling in Lopez de
Prado is a secondary classifier TRAINED ON THE PRIMARY MODEL'S REALISED
OUTCOMES; that is the entire point of the technique. This is a scoring rubric
wearing its name. Via enforceHardVetoes it has ABSOLUTE VETO AUTHORITY OVER
EVERY TRADE IN BOTH ROUTES.

Its two most influential inputs are equally unfounded:

  VPIN is computed on DAILY bars. Real VPIN buckets a volume clock. The
  bulk-volume classifier should normalise by sigma of price changes; the code
  uses MEAN ABSOLUTE CHANGE (~0.80 sigma for a normal) and then multiplies by an
  arbitrary 1.5. The 0.65 toxicity threshold is an absolute constant -- VPIN is
  conventionally read as a percentile of its own history, because its level is
  not comparable across instruments. That arbitrary constant is a hard veto.

  HURST is R/S over four lags with no Anis-Lloyd small-sample correction. R/S is
  badly upward-biased at these lengths and the standard error is roughly +/-0.1
  -- wider than the 0.45 / 0.65 decision bands it feeds.

Meanwhile the pooled upside model -- the one component that WAS trained,
walk-forward validated with an embargo, and honestly reported (IC 0.0203,

C-7  CORRELATED OBSERVATIONS ARE COUNTED AS INDEPENDENT -- IN BOTH SIGNIFICANCE
     TESTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
One statistical error, two places, both of which decide whether a trade is taken.

RUNG SELECTION
  tradeStats computes standardError = sigma / sqrt(netReturns.length) over
  FILLS. But the five rungs of one week share one anchor and one week's price
  move; they are close to a single observation. The effective sample is the
  number of WEEKS, not fills -- so the t-statistic is inflated by roughly
  sqrt(fills / weeks).

  The multiple-testing hurdle sqrt(2*ln(k)) ~ 1.89 is a defensible heuristic;
  the problem is that it is applied to an already-inflated statistic. Combined
  with requiredFills = 5 -- five observations to declare a rung tradable --
  noise clears the gate routinely.

MODEL SKILL
  upside.json reports informationCoefficientStandardError: 0.0068, which is
  1/sqrt(21429-3). But rows are NAMES x DATES, and on any given date ~500 Indian
  large caps share one market factor. The effective sample is the number of
  scored DATES -- order 10^2, not 10^4 -- so the true standard error is roughly
  an order of magnitude larger, plausibly 0.10-0.16.

  IC = 0.0203 IS THEN WELL INSIDE ONE STANDARD ERROR OF ZERO.

  Your model-governance.json already lists "enforce date-block resampling to
  account for cross-sectional stock correlation" as a promotion prerequisite,
  and your dossier raised it as P1-003. The file knows. The number printed next
  to it does not.

CREDIT WHERE DUE
  The governance layer reaches the right conclusion anyway -- the model is
  demoted, and the audit findings in that JSON are candid in a way most systems
  never are.

  BUT NOTE THE SIDE EFFECT: with canGenerateTrades: false, potentialCall stands
  aside unconditionally, so THE ENTIRE "POTENTIAL" ROUTE IS CURRENTLY DEAD. That
  may well be correct. It should be a deliberate decision, not a discovery.

C-8  FILLS ARE ASSUMED ON TOUCH, ON BOTH SIDES OF EVERY TRADE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    const filled = short ? session.high >= entry.price : session.low <= entry.price;
    const targetReached = short ? session.low <= lot.target : session.high >= lot.target;

A resting limit buy fills whenever the day's low TOUCHES it -- 100% of the time,
including when the low IS your price and the market never traded through. The
exit assumes the same. Entry and exit optimism therefore compound in the same
direction, and the effect is completely invisible to the test suite because the
tests supply synthetic bars constructed to trigger fills.

On a 2% target against ~0.06% modelled cost, fill realism is not a refinement --
it is most of the edge. At minimum: require trade-through (low < price,
strictly), or a volume-at-price condition, or a haircut calibrated against the
intraday feed you already have a module for.

A-3  LADDER THE RESIDUAL, NOT THE PRICE  [HIGHEST VALUE IDEA]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
weeklyCycle runs per symbol on raw price. So a stock down 6% while Nifty is down
6%, and a stock down 6% while Nifty is flat, are indistinguishable to this
system. Only the second is oversold. The first is just beta.

Equity mean reversion is overwhelmingly a CROSS-SECTIONAL phenomenon: reversal
works on residuals after removing the market and sector factor, not on raw
prices. Your ladder harvests whatever reversal survives in raw prices -- which
is mostly market noise. It is also why a dip-buying grid gets destroyed in a
drawdown: every name fills at once, because they are all filling on the same
hidden factor.

THE CHANGE
  1. Estimate each name's beta to Nifty, and ideally to its sector index.
  2. Compute the residual series: r_stock - beta * r_index.
  3. Hang the ladder off the RESIDUAL, not the price.
  4. Hedge the residual book's net beta with an index future.

This converts a directional dip-buying grid into statistical arbitrage, and it
removes the largest source of correlated loss in the book.

THE INPUTS ALREADY EXIST -- verified
  api/market.js:365   beta20 = covariance(stockRiskReturns, benchmarkRiskReturns)
                              / benchmarkVariance
  api/ai-run.js:36    benchmark = await fetchChart("^NSEI", { range: "5y" })

  MEASUREMENT: for each week, decompose ladder P&L into (factor component) +
  (residual component). If the edge lives in the residual, the hedged version
  should show a similar mean with materially lower variance. If the edge is
  entirely factor, you have been running a levered long-Nifty position with
  extra steps -- and that is worth knowing before you scale it.

A-5  YOU ARE SHORT GAMMA AND MEASURING IT WITH THE WRONG FAMILY OF STATISTICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Buy dips, sell 2% rallies, no stop. That is a structurally SHORT GAMMA payoff:
many small wins, rare large losses.

Every headline statistic in the system is from the wrong family for that shape:
hitRatePct, profitFactor, targetHits, and the Wilson lower bound on hit rate.
All are well implemented. All are least informative about the only thing that
can hurt you.

It is worse than uninformative. Rung selection filters on lowerPct >= 45 --
selecting on hit rate. For a short-gamma payoff THAT ACTIVELY SELECTS FOR TAIL
RISK, because the rungs with the best hit rates are the ones that mean-revert
reliably right up until they do not.

WHAT TO MEASURE INSTEAD
  * CVaR / expected shortfall at 95 and 99, per rung and per portfolio week.
  * Average win divided by WORST loss, not by average loss.
  * Ulcer index and time-under-water, which respect the path.
  * Omega ratio at a zero threshold, which uses the whole distribution.
  * MOST IMPORTANTLY: the explicit distribution of "the ladder went five deep
    and kept going." How often, how far past rung 5, how long to recover. That
    is the loss mode of this strategy and NOTHING in the repo estimates it.

  MEASUREMENT: build the depth-conditional survival curve. For every historical
  week where rung 5 filled, record the maximum adverse excursion beyond rung 5
  and the time to recovery. The tail of that curve is your true risk, and it is
  currently unmeasured, unbudgeted and unhedged.

This single reframe would change WHICH rungs the system selects.

READ THE HIT-RATE COLUMN AGAINST THE P&L COLUMN. They move in OPPOSITE

    stop widens  ->  P&L      FALLS +0.11% -> -0.17% per fill

This is the cleanest empirical proof available that the rung-selection gate this

admitted a rung on `Number(entry.lowerPct) >= 45` -- the Wilson lower bound on
HIT RATE. Optimise that column and you land on the no-stop cell: the best hit
rate in the table (58.0%), the worst expectancy (-0.1665%), and a worst single
trade of -61.84%. Gate on the tail instead (the w/l and CVaR columns, which is
what this round changed) and you land on the tight-stop cell that actually pays.

THE SHIPPED CONFIGURATION -- 2% step, 2% target, 2% stop -- IS THE BREAKEVEN
CELL. avgNet -0.0060% per fill, t = -0.11. Not a small edge. No edge.

The short side reproduces the identical structure with the opposite sign
(stop 1% -> 4%: hit rate 37.1% -> 51.8%, avgNet -0.0575% -> -0.3296%, all
significantly negative). Two independent sides, same monotonic inversion, is a
strong internal consistency check on the finding.

WHY THE TIGHT STOP WORKS, structurally
  Anchor 100, 2% step. Rung 1 fills at 98.00; a 1% stop sits at 97.02. Rung 2
  fills at 96.04. So rung 1 is stopped out BEFORE rung 2 fills. The tight stop
  dismantles the martingale: instead of averaging down into a falling name, the
  desk takes a sequence of independent 1%-risk / 2-3%-reward bets at a ~40% hit
  rate, which is positive expectancy. The 2-8% stops preserve the martingale,
  and the martingale is what was losing the money.

CAVEAT, stated plainly: the table above is in-sample over the full five years.
t = 4.23 against a hurdle deflated for 14 cells is strong, but a sweep result is
not evidence until it survives out of sample. See B-6.

B-3  WHAT I FIXED IN THE MEASUREMENT LAYER, AND WHAT IT WAS WORTH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All of the following are in api/_lib/weekly-cycle.js, on by default, and asserted
BEHAVIOURALLY by scripts/verify-ci-flags.js.

C-7  CLUSTER-ROBUST STANDARD ERRORS (clusterStandardError)
     Was: sigma / sqrt(fills), treating five rungs of one week as five
     independent observations. Now: the sandwich estimator clustering on the
     week, with the finite-cluster correction, and both figures reported next to
     an explicit standardErrorInflationFactor.
     HONEST MAGNITUDE: smaller than I predicted. Per-name inflation ran 1.00x to
     1.30x, not the ~3x I asserted -- because the ladder rarely goes deep. On
     RELIANCE, 64.8% of weeks fill zero rungs and 24.5% fill exactly one, so
     there is usually nothing to cluster. The correction is right in principle
     and modest in practice. I over-claimed its size in the first report.

C-8  TRADE-THROUGH FILLS (fillRequiresTradeThrough, default true)
     A resting limit order now requires the market to trade THROUGH the price,
     not merely touch it, on entries and on profit targets. Stops remain
     deliberately asymmetric and trigger on touch.
     HONEST MAGNITUDE: on real continuous prices this is very nearly a no-op,
     because exact equality between a computed rung price and a market low
     essentially never occurs. It is correct, it closes a real hole in synthetic
     tests, and it is NOT where fill realism actually bites. The real exposure is
     queue position and partial fills at a price the market did trade through
     briefly -- unmodelled, and the honest next step on execution realism.

A-5  TAIL-BASED RUNG SELECTION -- the change that mattered
     Was: fills >= 5 AND averageNet > 0 AND t >= hurdle AND lowerPct >= 45.
     Now: independent WEEKS >= requiredClusters AND averageNet > 0 AND
     cluster-robust t >= hurdle AND CVaR95 <= maxRungCvar95Pct AND
     avgWin/worstLoss >= minWinToWorstLossRatio.
     Hit rate is still reported; it no longer admits anything. Per B-0 this is
     the difference between selecting a profitable configuration and selecting
     the one with a -61.84% worst trade. Rejections are now itemised per rung in
     `rungRejections` so a stand-aside says which test failed.

EXIT POLICY MADE EXPLICIT AND MEASURABLE (exitPolicy, replayContinuous)
     "friday-flat" books every open leg at the Friday close. "continuous" carries
     legs across weekends, re-anchoring only NEW entries each Monday, and
     attributes weekly return as realised P&L plus the change in unrealised P&L
     so the weekly series still reconciles to lifetime P&L. See B-4.

ALSO: a real Sharpe ratio now nets a 6.5% risk-free rate; the prior figure did
not, so it was a return/vol ratio wearing a Sharpe label. Both are reported.
Under "continuous", annualisationValid is set false with the reason attached,
because carrying legs across weekends makes the weekly series serially
correlated and sqrt(52) then understates variance.
ADDED: maxDrawdownPct, timeUnderWaterPct, ulcerIndex, cvar95WeeklyPct, and
depthProfile() -- the distribution of how deep the ladder went, which is the
loss mode nothing in the repo had ever estimated.

B-4  THE EXIT-POLICY CONFLICT IS REAL AND IT IS YOURS TO SETTLE

Three artefacts in this system state three different rules for the same
parameter, and it is the parameter that determines what every statistic means:

  YOUR STATED DESK RULE   "everything booked at the Friday close"
  THE PAYLOAD             recommendation.js sets bookOut to "Hold open legs
                          continuously across weekends with Monday reference
                          rebase; exit strictly on target, stop, or the

  THE BACKTEST            replayWeek() books outcome "friday" at the close

8.712% vs 8.426%, and a longest hold of 15 sessions against 4. On that single
name, carrying risk over the weekend bought no return and added tail. Confirm on
the pooled panel before deciding.

cut, then scored on the block that follows with no further adjustment. THE
SELECTION IS INSIDE THE TEST, which is the only honest way to evaluate a
parameter that came out of a sweep. Grid of 10 geometries (stops 1/2/3/4/none x
targets 2/3). t-statistics cluster-robust on the week.

WHAT THIS IS WORTH IN MONEY -- do not skip this part
  +0.1894% per fill is the per-TRADE figure, and participation is low: roughly
  0.62 fills per name-week across the panel. Capital-weighted at 1/5 per rung,
  the pooled sweep put the 1%/3% cell at about +0.0258% per name-week, which is
  near 1.3% per year on the notional allocated to a name, or roughly 6% per year
  on margin posted at ~22%. Statistically real. Economically modest.

  The binding constraint is therefore NOT the signal any more -- it is
  participation and capital efficiency. That reorders the improvement list in
  Appendix A: volatility-normalised grid geometry (A-4) now matters most, because
  a fixed 2% step is exactly what suppresses fill frequency on low-volatility
  names, and cross-sectional residual laddering (A-3) matters next, because it
  raises the number of genuinely independent bets rather than the size of each.

     the grid boundaries were informed by prior looking. A fully clean test
     specifies the grid before seeing any result.
  2. ONE REGIME. Five years of a broadly rising Indian large-cap market. A
     mean-reversion ladder with a tight stop has never been tested here through a
     sustained bear market or a liquidity event.
  3. EXECUTION IS STILL OPTIMISTIC. Costs are a modelled 0.08%. Queue position,
     partial fills and impact are unmodelled, and at a 3% target on a 1% stop the

  5. 40 LARGE CAPS. Not the 214-name F&O universe, and survivorship is not
