This is not a flat line with noise around it. It rises to 2023, decays, and
turns significantly negative in 2026. A t of -2.10 is past the conventional 5%
two-sided threshold in the WRONG direction: the rule is not merely unprofitable
in 2026, it is losing money at a rate unlikely to be chance.

And it independently replicates the earlier failure. The original 97-symbol
study showed +0.0613% at t 0.747 in its final window. These 112 entirely
different names show -0.0108% at t -0.15 over the same calendar window. Two
disjoint samples, same answer: whatever worked earlier is gone now.

That agreement is what makes this a finding rather than an unlucky draw.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT I DID NOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The held-out sample DOES contain a window that passes. Split by the same
calendar boundaries as the earlier study, the middle window on these fresh names
returns +0.3283% per fill at t 4.32, deflated 2.66 -- comfortably through the
hurdle. Reporting that as the headline would have been a clean-looking pass.

It would also have been exactly the manoeuvre the protocol forbade: "splitting
the 113 into sub-groups until one of them passes". The pooled figure is the
honest statistic because it is the only one that was not selected after seeing
the alternatives. I am recording the passing window here so that nobody
discovers it later and wonders why it went unmentioned.

No parameter was changed. No symbol was dropped. No variant was run and
discarded. The sample is now spent: it has been looked at, so it can never serve
as a held-out test again.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ESTABLISHED
  - The momentum-breakout rule does not generalise to unseen names at a level
    that survives its own selection hurdle.
  - Its performance decays over time and is significantly negative in 2026 on
    fresh names.
  - The earlier final-test failure was not sampling noise; it reproduces.

NOT ESTABLISHED
  - This is a fresh CROSS-SECTION, not a fresh calendar period, because no
    future data exists. Regime overlaps with windows already seen. A common
    factor could affect both, and the 2026 result in particular rests on a
    single partial year.
  - The 113 held-out names skew mid-cap against the original large-cap 97.
    Mid-caps are more volatile and costlier to trade. No adjustment was made,
    which was stated in advance, but it means the comparison is not perfectly
    clean.
  - THE DEPLOYED ENGINE WAS NOT TESTED. What failed here is the momentum rule in
    scripts/apex-panel-study.js. The percentile-ranked engine actually serving
    k-square31.onrender.com has still never been backtested at all. It is not
    exonerated by this result and it is not condemned by it. It is untested.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHAT FOLLOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The rule as specified should not be traded. Not "should be monitored", not
"needs more data" -- it had 8,893 fills across 112 names and five years, which is
not a small-sample problem.

If the rule is to be revised, the revision cannot be tested on this sample or on
the original 97. Both are spent. It must be tested on forward data collected
after the change is made, which means writing the new rule down, stamping it,
and waiting.

The more useful next step is the one this test exposed: the engine that is
actually deployed has never been measured. That is the gap to close, and it
should be closed with a pre-registered protocol like this one, written before
the run rather than after.
