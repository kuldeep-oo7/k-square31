1. WHAT THIS IS
================================================================================

A trading desk for Indian equity derivatives (NSE F&O). It scans 210 F&O
underlyings, builds 423 option cards (call and put per name plus two indices),
prices them, and tells the user what to buy, at what price, where to sell, where
to stop, and how long it should take.

  Live URL      https://k-square31.onrender.com
  Repository    github.com/kuldeep-oo7/k-square31, branch main
  Owner         Kuldeep (kuldeepsavaliya70@gmail.com)
  Size          ~36,500 lines across api/, scripts/, site/, server.js
  Commits       241
  Language      Node.js (CommonJS, no framework, no build step)

WHO IT IS FOR: one user, trading his own money, on a phone during market hours.
He is not a quant. He has asked repeatedly for plain instructions — buy at X,
sell at Y, stop at Z, how long — and every time the desk answered with a lecture
instead, he was right to be angry. Keep the surface small.

WHAT IT IS FOR: pure TRADING, not investment. Intraday to a few weeks.


================================================================================
2. THE ONE RULE
================================================================================

    A number the system MADE UP must never be presented as a number the
    system MEASURED.

Every serious defect in this repository's history has that exact shape. Not
most. All of them. Section 9 is the catalogue, and it is worth reading in full
before writing code, because the failure keeps arriving in new clothes:

  - a seeded track record served as audited performance
  - a hash function standing in for a price feed
  - a sine wave standing in for a score
  - the sum of hypothetical gains labelled "Purchased Trades MTM"
  - a fetch latency labelled as a quote age
  - a model's own input volatility compared against realized volatility
  - exit prices computed from a premium the card refused to display

The practical rules that follow from it:

  1. ABSENCE IS NOT ZERO. "No closed signals" and "a 0% win rate" are different
     facts. Return null, render an em-dash, never a placeholder number.
  2. EVERY PRICE CARRIES ITS PROVENANCE. A live broker book, an exchange settled
     close, and a model output must never render identically.
  3. FAIL CLOSED. No credentials means NOT_CONFIGURED and no data, never a
     fallback estimate. A guess reaches a card with an order button on it.
  4. A TEST THAT ASSERTS NOTHING MUST NOT LOOK LIKE A TEST THAT PASSED. An
     earlier check reported "0 mismatches" across 565 contracts while checking
     zero of them, because the field it compared was never stored.

3. WHAT IS PROVEN AND WHAT IS NOT
================================================================================

READ THIS BEFORE BELIEVING ANY OUTPUT.

PROVEN (measured, reproducible, tested):
  - Contract identity. 565 of 565 cached contracts match the broker's own
    instrument label on underlying, expiry, strike and right.
  - Put-call parity holds: slope -0.995, R² 0.9999 across 12 symbols. Calls and
    puts are not transposed.
  - Open interest agrees with the NSE bhavcopy on 487 of 500 contracts.
  - At-the-money premiums sit 2.05% (median) from the exchange settled price.
  - Lot sizes agree with the bhavcopy across 659 contracts, zero disagreements.
  - Black-Scholes matches Hull's published values: call 4.7594 and put 0.8086
    to within 2.3e-5 and 2.3e-8; all five Greeks to three decimals; put-call
    parity with dividends to 7.11e-15; IV round trip to 2.79e-10.
  - The arithmetic of every vertical spread payoff at expiry.
  - The volatility surface is coherent once priced on the future: RICH 98,
    FAIR 160, CHEAP 102, EXPENSIVE 60 — implied above realized, as real option
    markets behave — with IV spanning 13.6% to 85%, median 23.5%.
  - A simulated trading day (`npm run rehearse`) passes 34 checks against moving
    prices: a wilder stock raises P(touch) AND shortens time to target, a
    dropped feed leaves the last real mark alone, and a loss is recorded as
    exactly (exit - entry) x lot.

NOT PROVEN — THE SIGNAL DOES NOT HAVE A MEASURED EDGE:
  - Pre-registered out-of-sample test: 112 symbols never touched in development,
    8,893 fills, +0.0422% per fill, t = 1.25 against a 1.6648 hurdle. FAILED.
  - By year: 2023 +0.1358% (peak), 2026 -0.1752% at t = -2.10. Negative and
    significant in the most recent year.
  - 0 of 24 ladder geometries survived validation.
  - P(profit) across the 423 option cards: 23% to 44.6%, median 35%. Not one
    card is above 45%.

So: the MACHINERY is trustworthy and the SELECTION is not. The desk can tell you
exactly what a contract is, what it costs, what it pays and what the odds are.
It cannot yet tell you that buying it makes money. Do not let any future feature
blur that line, and do not let a good week be mistaken for evidence.

NEVER OBSERVED: the REALTIME (executable) quote path has never been seen during
an open market session — all the work happened after 15:30. See section 11.

6. HOW THE DATA FLOWS
================================================================================

BOOT (realtime-market-engine.js bootstrap(), strictly ordered — each step needs
the one before it, and firing them in parallel makes the first quote refresh
lose a race it should not be in):

  1. refreshContractReference()  NSE bhavcopy -> strikes, lots, settled prices.
     CRITICAL: NSE publishes the bhavcopy only AFTER the close. Asking for
     TODAY's file during a live session always 404s. It walks back up to 7 days
     to the most recent available session. Asking only for today was why the
     desk spent an entire trading day with no prices at all.
  2. refreshDhanScrips() and refreshBrokerInstruments() in parallel.
  3. maintainBrokerSessions()    Angel logs in or refreshes; Upstox is watched.
  4. refreshLiveQuotes()         Option chains for the recommended names.
  5. restoreFromRemote()         Pull the ledger back if the disk was wiped.

TIMERS: quotes and ledger every 90s, sessions hourly, reference every 6h, tick
cycle every 4s.

PER REQUEST (/api/state):
  engine.getUniverse()  -> cached 2.5s, keyed on macro state identity
  -> universe rows (nested option chain STRIPPED from the wire: it was being
     sent twice, 1.1 MB of a 4.6 MB payload)
  -> options universe (423 flattened cards)
  -> gzip when the client asks: 3.5 MB becomes 244 KB, a 93% reduction
  The WHOLE handler is inside one try/catch. It did not used to be, and a single
  unformattable date returned 502 for the entire desk.

PRICING WATERFALL — each step down is LABELLED, never silently substituted:

    LIVE_BROKER_QUOTE      mid of a two-sided Angel book, inside 120s
    BROKER_CLOSING_BOOK    today's book, market shut, NOT executable
    EXCHANGE_SETTLED_PRICE the bhavcopy close
    NO_TRADED_PRICE        refused
    (a model price NEVER reaches a card as a tradeable premium)

THE UNDERLYING WATERFALL — read this before touching any pricing code:

    LIVE_BROKER_FUTURE         Angel's live futures quote        208 of 210 cards
    EXCHANGE_SETTLED_FUTURE    the bhavcopy futures price        the 2 indices
    CASH_SPOT_NO_FUTURE        the equity share price            LAST RESORT

AN INDIAN STOCK OPTION IS AN OPTION ON THE FUTURE. Pricing one against the cash
share is not a rounding error. Measured on APOLLOHOSP on 2026-09-20: cash
8944.38 against a future of 8865, a gap of 0.90%, which turned an at-the-money
implied volatility of 13.74% into 6.02% — understated by 7.72 points — and
produced a smile ramping 6% to 13% across four strikes, a shape no real option
market makes. Across the universe the cash share is off by a median of 0.77% and
up to 3.07%, and THE SIGN FLIPS PER NAME (COFORGE -2.50%, RELIANCE +1.22%), so
it does not cancel: it distorted every symbol differently and therefore
distorted the ranking BETWEEN them.

Put-call parity gives the market's own forward with no model at all, and is the
check to use if you ever doubt this:  F = K + (C - P)·e^(rT).  On APOLLOHOSP it
returned 8869.44, 8871.19 and 8870.10 from three different strikes while the
cash share said 8944.38. The options were telling us the right number.

Every card carries `pricingBasis` and `pricingUnderlying` so this is auditable.


================================================================================
7. THE FRESHNESS CONTRACT (live-quotes.js)
================================================================================

The most subtle correct thing in the codebase. Do not simplify it.

THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES: a quote fetched at 21:48 IST was reporting an age of two
seconds. The two seconds were real — that is how long the REQUEST took. The book
carried the exchange's own stamp of 15:40, six hours earlier, and the contract
had last traded six days before that. Retrieval time was being presented as
quote time.

THE EXCHANGE CLOCK: Angel sends exchFeedTime as "17-Sep-2026 15:40:00" with NO
offset. Date.parse reads that in the HOST's timezone — correct on an IST laptop,
five and a half hours out on Render, where during market hours it places the
quote in the FUTURE, and a negative age passes every "older than" test. So a
stale book would read as fresh on precisely the machine that serves real users.
The offset is applied EXPLICITLY. Tests assert an identical verdict under UTC,
IST and US/Eastern hosts, and are mutation-checked.

Also: 01-Jan-1970 05:30:00 is epoch zero expressed in IST — the exchange's way
of saying a contract has never traded. It is a sentinel, not a time, and is
refused rather than returned as a 56-year-old quote.

THREE STATES, not a boolean:

  REALTIME      exchange priced it inside 120s, market open. EXECUTABLE.
  CLOSING_BOOK  today's book, market shut. NOT executable, but KEPT — its
                spread is a measured spread, and discarding it switched the
                spread gate off at 15:30 every day, which is exactly when the
                desk gets read.
  STALE         not from today, or no exchange clock at all. Upstox sends no
                feed time, so its books can never claim REALTIME. Absent
                evidence must not read as evidence of currency.

MARKET PHASE comes from the CLOCK (09:15–15:30 IST, weekends and pre-open
excluded), never from a quote. It used to be read off the live book, so with no
credentials there was no quote, the phase was undefined, and every card printed
"MARKET CLOSED" at 09:50 on a trading day.

8. THE MATHS — EVERY FORMULA, WHERE IT LIVES, WHETHER IT IS USED
================================================================================

OPTION PRICING (api/_lib/option-pricing.js)
  Black-Scholes-Merton     C = S·e^(-qT)·N(d1) - K·e^(-rT)·N(d2)
  THE UNDERLYING           An Indian stock option is an option ON THE FUTURE,
                           and everything is priced on the live futures quote.
                           See the underlying waterfall in section 6. Using the
                           cash share understated at-the-money implied
                           volatility by 7.72 points on APOLLOHOSP and by a
                           median of 0.77% of underlying across the universe,
                           with the SIGN FLIPPING per name so it never cancels.
  Put-call parity          C - P = (F - K)e^(-rT). Used two ways: as an identity
                           check that calls and puts are not transposed (slope
                           -0.995, R² 0.9999), and to recover THE MARKET'S OWN
                           FORWARD with no model at all, F = K + (C - P)e^(rT).
                           That is the tool to reach for whenever an implied
                           volatility looks wrong.
  Greeks                   delta, gamma, theta, vega, rho; verified against
                           finite differences.
  Implied volatility       bisection, 200 iterations, 1e-8 tolerance, bracketed
                           and bounded. Refuses outside no-arbitrage bounds.
  Put-call parity          C - P = (F - K)·e^(-rT). Used as an INDEPENDENT
                           identity check: slope of (C-P) on K must be near -1.
  Normal CDF               Abramowitz & Stegun 26.2.17, |error| < 7.5e-8.

VOLATILITY (api/_lib/trade-odds.js)
  Close-to-close RV        σ = stdev(log returns) · sqrt(252)
  Parkinson RV             σ = sqrt( mean(ln(H/L)²) / (4·ln2) ) · sqrt(252)
                           ~5x more efficient; a stock that travels widely and
                           closes flat shows nothing in a close-to-close series.
  Variance risk premium    IV / RV, with the realized window MATCHED TO THE
                           OPTION'S TENOR — comparing a 20-day backward estimate
                           with a 9-day forward implied volatility measures two
                           different horizons. IV is solved with the time to
                           expiry that applied WHEN THE PREMIUM WAS STRUCK, not
                           now, or a weekend inflates every reading by 1.3 to
                           1.9 points. THE number retail never sees and the one
                           that most reliably decides the outcome. An option's
                           price is a price for future MOVEMENT: paying 32%
                           implied on a stock realizing 23% means overpaying by
                           a third, and being right about DIRECTION does not
                           repair paying the wrong price for VOLATILITY.
                           Verdicts: >=1.25 EXPENSIVE, >=1.10 RICH, >=0.90 FAIR,
                           else CHEAP. Measured across 204 cards: 23 CHEAP,
                           71 FAIR, 63 RICH, 47 EXPENSIVE.
  CRITICAL: IV must be SOLVED FROM THE TRADED PREMIUM. greeks.iv is the
  volatility fed INTO the model and is carried in PERCENT. Comparing it with
  realized compares an assumption with a measurement and returns whatever was
  assumed — it read 2800% and EXPENSIVE on every card until fixed.

PROBABILITY (api/_lib/trade-odds.js)
  P(finish beyond)         N(d2) with the stock's OWN realized volatility, not
                           implied — implied is the price being charged, and
                           using it quotes the seller's assumptions back as
                           though they were evidence.
  P(touch before expiry)   2·N(-|ln(B/S)| / (σ·sqrt(T))) by reflection. The
                           position can be closed the moment the underlying
                           TOUCHES the level, so first passage is the right
                           question, not the end point.
  Median time to touch     the same expression solved for the t at which half
                           the eventual touches have occurred.
  DRIFT IS ZERO, DELIBERATELY. Any non-zero drift is a directional forecast and
  this desk has no validated one. Inserting one would smuggle an assumed edge
  into a number presented as a measurement.

PORTFOLIO (api/_lib/covariance.js)
  Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage    toward a constant-correlation target, δ solved
                           analytically. Recovers a known covariance; PSD across
                           400 random probes; δ adapts with sample length.
  Euler risk decomposition risk contributions sum to portfolio volatility.
  Meucci effective bets    entropy over PRINCIPAL PORTFOLIOS, not a Herfindahl
                           of risk contributions. 16 independent assets read
                           15.65; perfectly correlated assets read 1.0.
  Principal components     power iteration with deflation; PC1 share matches a
                           closed form to 45.00%.

STATISTICS (api/_lib/panel.js)
  Cluster-robust errors    sandwich estimator, clustered on calendar week.
  Purged calendar splits   labels straddling a boundary are DROPPED, not leaked.
  Deflated Sharpe          Bailey & López de Prado expected maximum of N draws
                           as the selection hurdle. With trials:1 the deflation
                           is a no-op — that mistake turned an honest t of 0.77
                           into a reported 2.43.
  Pre-registration         protocol and decision rule committed BEFORE the test.

LIQUIDITY AND RISK
  fno-liquidity.js         ADV, OI, capacity in whole lots, annualised basis
                           (median 10.64%).
  advanced-quant.js        GARCH(1,1) conditional volatility, Cornish-Fisher
                           VaR (validity requires the expansion to stay
                           monotone — it silently returned 0 on fat tails until
                           that was checked), CVaR, Kyle's lambda, Amihud.
  portfolio-risk.js        Order schema, concentration, leverage, stress, a net
                           loss kill switch.

MACHINE LEARNING — HONESTLY LABELLED
  ml-model.js              Deterministic gradient boosting, leak-free labelling,
                           walk-forward validation, a trust gate that rejects
                           noise. Governance DEMOTES weak models to advisory
                           only; they cannot generate trades standalone.
  upside.js                Pooled cross-sectional model. Per-name fits are
                           REFUSED as unvalidatable.
  neural-quant-engine.js   Scores cards. The CNN is UNTRAINED and labelled as
                           such. Do not present its output as a prediction.
  VPIN                     returns null for every name. Not implemented.

9. THE DEFECT CATALOGUE — READ THIS ONE TWICE
================================================================================

Every entry is a real defect that reached the user. They are listed because the
same shapes keep coming back, and recognising the shape is faster than
rediscovering the instance.

FABRICATION
  - seedDefaultData() inserted a 71.4% win rate and Rs 51,492.50 of profit, and
    served them from /api/state as audited performance. TWO TEST SUITES
    ASSERTED THE FICTION rather than catching it.
  - The Apex engine priced from a hash function and scored from a sine wave.
  - "Purchased Trades MTM: +Rs 51,770.75" was the sum of hypothetical gains
    across 31 RECOMMENDATIONS nobody had bought, in the largest green type on
    the page, above a ledger correctly reporting zero.
  - While building the paper ledger, the assistant RECREATED this defect: save()
    persisted whatever store it was handed, so test fixtures landed in the live
    ledger and the desk reported one closed trade, a 100% win rate and Rs 9,840
    that nobody traded. A test now asserts the live DB is untouched, byte for
    byte, after the suite runs.

WRONG UNITS, WRONG CLOCK, WRONG LABEL
  - Retrieval latency presented as quote age (2 seconds vs 6 hours).
  - Date.parse on an offset-less exchange timestamp: correct on an IST laptop,
    5.5 hours wrong on Render, placing quotes in the FUTURE where negative age
    passes every staleness check.
  - greeks.iv (percent, model input) compared against realized volatility
    (decimal, measured). Every card read EXPENSIVE at 2800%.
  - getDaysToMonthlyExpiry() returns an OBJECT, not a number, and assumes a last
    Thursday. NSE moved stock F&O off Thursday.
  - Angel trading symbols are DAY-first (AMBER29SEP265800CE = 29 SEP 26, strike
    5800). Parsed year-first — the Zerodha convention — it reported 1130
    mismatches that did not exist.
  - Angel strikes arrive in PAISE.

INCONSISTENT GATES
  - entryPrice gated on legPriced, exits gated on the raw premium. Cards printed
    "BUY Rs null" beside "SELL Rs 16.7" and "STOP Rs 5.57" — exits for a trade
    with no entry.
  - The spread gate keyed on "is there a LIVE quote", so it switched itself off
    at 15:30 and a 45.66% spread went back to available.
  - marketPhase read off the live book, so no credentials meant every card said
    "MARKET CLOSED" during an open session.

CRASHES AND CAPACITY
  - new Date(NaN).toISOString() throws. /api/state had no outer try/catch, so
    one unformattable BAR-AGE field returned 502 for the whole desk during live
    market hours.
  - refreshReference asked for exactly one date — today — and gave up on a 404.
    NSE publishes after the close, so the one date guaranteed to fail was the
    only one requested. No strikes, no lots, no prices for a whole session.
  - getUniverse() recomputed 210 names on EVERY caller and /api/state called it
    three times per request. 313 MB RSS on a 512 MB host.
  - The same option chain was serialised twice: nested on every universe row and
    again as the array the page renders from.

WRONG INPUT, RIGHT FORMULA — the 2026-09-20 audit
  - OPTIONS WERE PRICED AGAINST THE CASH SHARE, NOT THE FUTURE. The single
    largest correctness defect found to date. An Indian stock option is an
    option on the future; pricing it on the equity understated at-the-money
    implied volatility by 7.72 points on APOLLOHOSP (6.02% where the truth was
    13.74%) and produced a smile ramping 6% to 13% across four strikes, a shape
    no real market makes. The SYMPTOM was visible and easy to miss: the top five
    recommendations all came back CHEAP, and the whole universe read CHEAP 154
    against EXPENSIVE 122 — implied BELOW realized, which is the reverse of how
    option markets behave. Because the desk ranks on cheap-versus-expensive, it
    was systematically selecting the names whose underlying error was largest.
    THE LESSON: when a verdict is lopsided across a whole universe, suspect the
    input, not the market. Put-call parity recovers the true forward with no
    model at all and would have caught this in one line.
  - TWO GUARDS THAT HAD NEVER FIRED. The fundamentals cache directory did not
    exist, so getCachedFundamentals returned null for all 210 names. The
    earnings veto (fund?.events?.insideTradingWeek) was undefined every time —
    so the desk could recommend buying premium into an earnings announcement,
    the one setup where being right about direction still loses. The
    balance-sheet penalty never fired either: 21 names carry debt-to-equity over
    200, CHOLAFIN at 695, all ranked as though clean. Both read as working code.
    Neither had data. LOOK FOR THIS SHAPE: a guard whose input is never
    populated is worse than no guard, because nobody looks for it.
  - A FIFTH OF THE COMPOSITE SCORE WAS TWO CONSTANTS. chart and vpin took the
    same value (50) for all 210 names, so they added ten points to everything
    and ranked nothing, while the published componentWeights claimed they did
    20% of the work. The formula also binarised hurst, collapsing 160 distinct
    values onto {50, 85} across a single threshold.
  - 972 OF 5,791 SETTLED CALLS ARE PRICED BELOW INTRINSIC VALUE, across 168
    symbols — POWERINDIA 26000 CE settled at 4,162 against an intrinsic of
    6,699. NSE derives settlement prices for contracts that did not trade. None
    reach a card (the desk picks near the money) but an implied volatility
    solved from one is meaningless, so they are refused explicitly.
  - THE REALIZED WINDOW DID NOT MATCH THE TENOR. A 20-day backward volatility
    was being compared with a 9-day forward implied volatility and the

10. THE DATABASE
================================================================================

SQLite via node:sqlite (experimental warning is expected), at
.private/k2-trading.db. WAL mode. ONE store — a second store means two answers
to the same question and eventually they disagree.

  account             equity and aggregate statistics. Exactly one row, ZEROED.
  positions           open positions.
  trades              closed trades.
  signals_lifecycle   THE PAPER LEDGER. See below.
  macro_snapshots     macro radar history.
  ticks_history       per-symbol tick history.
  audit_events        refusals and notable events, including back-date refusals.

signals_lifecycle carries id, symbol, trading_symbol, option_type, strike,
lot_size, entry_price, current_price, target1/2, stop_loss, issued_at,
closed_at, status, exit_reason, pnl_*, plus the provenance columns added
2026-09-20: entry_price_source, exit_price_source, entry_executable,
probability_of_profit_pct, volatility_verdict, expiry. SQLite has no ADD COLUMN
IF NOT EXISTS, so the migration reads PRAGMA table_info first and is idempotent.

THE LEDGER'S GUARANTEES, none of which are negotiable:
  - entry_price is written ONCE and never appears in a SET clause.
  - Every price carries its source. A live book and a model must not look alike.
  - Back-dated and future-dated signals are REFUSED and written to audit_events.
  - A position closes only against an OBSERVED price.
  - The win rate counts CLOSED rows only. Absence reports null, never zero.
  - Recording happens ONLY while the market is open. A signal issued against a
    closing book is not one anyone could have acted on.
  - A modelled premium can never open a position, or the ledger would be
    measuring the model against itself.

DURABILITY: the disk is ephemeral, so rows mirror to the Google Apps Script
store — a BACKUP of the one store, not a second store; nothing reads it except
the restore path. The merge is a union by id where a CLOSED row is never
reopened by an open snapshot. Currently INACTIVE: the two Google env vars are
not set.

VERIFICATION HABITS THAT WERE LEARNED THE HARD WAY:
  - ENUMERATE, never sample. The four-symbol spot check missed 343 wrong lots.
  - Cross-check against an INDEPENDENT source: the broker's own label, the
    exchange bhavcopy, or an identity like put-call parity.
  - Mutation-test the important fixes. Break the fix deliberately and confirm
    the test fails. A test that passes against the bug is decoration.
  - Assert that a check actually checked something. Count what you compared and
    assert the count.
  - Measure memory and payload for anything on the per-request path.
  - Kill stray `node server.js` processes. Six were once left running, each
    refreshing the same cache on a 90s timer with stale in-memory code,
    overwriting a field that had just been added and burning API quota.

COMMUNICATION WITH THIS USER:
  - He wants four things on a card: buy price, sell price, stop, how long. The
    card was once sixty lines of correct, unreadable prose. A card nobody
    finishes is worse than a card that says less.
  - Give him prices that map to a BUTTON in his broker app. "Sell the spread at
    Rs 1.82" maps to no field anywhere and is therefore a wrong answer however
    correct the arithmetic.
  - He is on a phone, on mobile data, often at the office. Do not assume he can
    run a script on his laptop.
  - Tell him what is broken before he finds it. He has caught real defects this
    system's own audits missed, and he is right to be angry when a number is
    presented as measured that was not.
  - Never dress up the failed out-of-sample result. He asked for the test, he
    accepted the answer, and the honest record is the product.
