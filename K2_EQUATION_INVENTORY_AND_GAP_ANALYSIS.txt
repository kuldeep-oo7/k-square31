PART 2 — THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE INVENTORY
================================================================================

You asked whether we are USING all of this. Here is the single decision that
every recommendation on the live desk passes through, verbatim from
api/_lib/unified-quant-scanner.js:147 --

    compScore = neuralAlphaScore                              * 0.35
              + candleResult.score * 100                      * 0.15
              + (chartResult.dominantPattern ? 80 : 50)       * 0.10
              + (hurst > 0.55 ? 85 : 50)                      * 0.15
              + (vpin.vpin < 0.25 ? 85 : 50)                  * 0.10
              + (garch.volatilityRegime === "MODERATE" ? 85 : 50) * 0.15

Read the last four terms. The Hurst exponent is estimated to four decimals and
then collapsed to EIGHTY-FIVE OR FIFTY. A name with Hurst 0.551 and a name with
Hurst 0.950 score identically. VPIN, the chart-pattern detector and the entire
GARCH volatility model are all reduced the same way, to a two-valued switch.

So the answer to "are we using all this maths" is: we COMPUTE it and then throw
almost all of it away at the moment of decision. Six inputs reach the gate, four
of them carrying one bit of information each. Cornish-Fisher VaR, CVaR, the
Kalman beta, Kelly, Amihud, the basis and the capacity model reach the gate not
at all -- they are displayed, and the decision is made without them.

That is the gap between having the equations and having a system.

PART 3 — TECHNICAL ANALYSIS, CANDLESTICKS AND CHART PATTERNS
================================================================================

You asked specifically whether we use these the way the big funds do.

WHAT WE HAVE. candlestick-engine.js and chart-patterns.js are real, are wired
into the scanner, and together carry 25% of the composite score -- the largest
single block after the neural term. Candlesticks enter as a continuous score;
chart patterns enter as the binary 80-or-50 described above.

WHAT THE LARGE FUNDS ACTUALLY DO WITH THEM, AND WHERE WE DIFFER

  1. They do not trust a pattern because it has a name. They measure it. For
     every "morning star" ever printed they compute the forward return
     distribution against a matched control, and they keep the pattern only if
     the difference survives out of sample. WE HAVE NEVER MEASURED A SINGLE
     PATTERN'S FORWARD RETURN. Ours are detected and scored on the assumption
     that the textbook is right.

  2. They use patterns as CONDITIONERS, not signals. A pattern that fires in a
     high-volatility regime, at a volume-profile value area, in a name with
     rising short interest, is a different object from the same shape in a dead
     tape. We apply one threshold to all 210 names in all conditions.

  3. They run multi-timeframe confirmation. A daily pattern disagreeing with the
     weekly is usually noise. We look at one timeframe.

  4. They anchor to volume-at-price -- VWAP, value areas, volume profile --
     because that is where real execution lives. We use volume only as a raw
     series and a 20-day ratio.

  5. Renaissance-style shops do not use candlesticks at all in the retail sense.
     They search enormous feature spaces for statistical regularities, and a
     "pattern" survives only as a fitted, out-of-sample-validated feature. The
     shape is incidental; the evidence is everything.

  6. They measure decay. Every published pattern is arbitraged over time. Our
     own pre-registered test just showed that behaviour precisely: the momentum
     rule returned +0.1358% per fill in 2023 and -0.1752% at t -2.10 in 2026.
     That is a public edge being competed away, which is what happens to any
     rule simple enough to name.

So: we HAVE technical analysis. We do not have the thing that makes it work at
a large fund, which is not better patterns -- it is the measurement apparatus
that tells you which patterns are real and when they stop being real.

================================================================================
PART 4 — WHAT I WOULD BUILD, IN ORDER
================================================================================

1. A COVARIANCE MATRIX. The single largest hole. Without it there is no CAPM, no
   beta, no factor model, no portfolio optimisation, no correlation-aware sizing,
   and no way to know whether 31 positions are 31 bets or 1. Everything in 1.4
   unlocks from this one object.

2. STOP COLLAPSING CONTINUOUS MATHS TO BINARY SWITCHES. Rank Hurst, VPIN and
   GARCH cross-sectionally the way neuralScore and compositeScore already are,
   so a four-decimal estimate contributes four decimals of information.

3. MEASURE EVERY CANDLESTICK AND CHART PATTERN. Forward return distribution per
   pattern against a matched control, out of sample. Keep what survives, delete
   what does not. This is a week of work and it is the highest-value week
   available, because it converts an assumption into evidence.

4. FIT THE GARCH PARAMETERS BY MAXIMUM LIKELIHOOD instead of holding alpha 0.08
   and beta 0.88 fixed for every instrument in the universe.

5. AMERICAN OPTION PRICING via a binomial tree. Indian single-stock options are
   American and we price them European; the error is concentrated in exactly the
   deep in-the-money puts where early exercise has value.

6. USE KELLY OR DELETE IT. A computed-and-ignored number is how a system starts
   believing things it has not done.

7. NORMALISE VPIN by sigma and read it as a percentile against its own history,
   so its level means the same thing in RELIANCE and in a mid-cap.

================================================================================
PART 5 — THE ONE SENTENCE
================================================================================

We have more of the canon implemented than most retail systems and most of it is
correct, verified against textbook constants and no-arbitrage identities. What we
do not have is the part that turns equations into a desk: a covariance matrix, a
decision that uses the precision it computes, and evidence that any of the
patterns predict anything. The equations are the vocabulary. The measurement is
the argument, and the argument is what has been missing.
