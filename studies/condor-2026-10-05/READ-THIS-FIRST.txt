NIFTY IRON CONDOR TEST, 5 Oct 2026 -- plain words

Question: the naked NIFTY 2% strangle (sell a call 2% up and a put 2% down each week) was the desk's
best result. It has unlimited crash risk. Does a HEDGED version (also buy a cheaper call and put
further out, an "iron condor") still make money?

Answer: NO. All three hedged versions FAILED. Rules were locked and pushed first (commit af9ce97),
then run once on NSE's own NIFTY option prices, Sep 2021 - Sep 2026, after charges and slippage.

  IC1 fixed 2% / 4% condor        +0.030% of NIFTY a week, t 1.75, 84% winning weeks, 247 trades  FAIL
  IC2 market's own range          +0.003% a week, t 0.15, 68% winning weeks                       FAIL
  IC3 IC2 + calm-market brake     +0.016% a week, t 0.70, 67% winning weeks                       FAIL
  (pass needs t >= 3)

For comparison, the naked 2% strangle with the same costs: +0.099% a week, t 4.57, but its worst
week lost 3.22% of NIFTY (about Rs 54,000 on one 75-unit lot at NIFTY 22,500), against 1.77% for the
condor.

What it means: the buyers of far-out NIFTY options pay about the same "insurance premium" the
strangle seller collects. Buying the wings hands back roughly 70% of the edge, and what is left is
too small to tell apart from luck. The edge is in carrying crash risk; remove the risk and the
edge goes with it.

Nothing goes to the page or to TRACK from this study. The naked strangle stays on TRACK as
information only (it needs margin and can lose months of gains in one week).
